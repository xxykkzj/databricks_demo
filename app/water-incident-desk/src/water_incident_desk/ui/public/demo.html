Incident & Report Desk

One place for major water network events, with the report beside each record and a status that people decide and the system remembers.

A Databricks App built in one workshop session, on synthetic data for Torrens Gulf Water, a fictional utility set in real South Australian places.

Major main breaks Storm sewer overflows Low-chlorine cases Leak alerts

Major events live in five places. Reports go missing between them.

A typical utility keeps each piece somewhere different:

  • Work orders and crew notes in the maintenance system
  • Flows and night-flow data from telemetry
  • Chlorine and E. coli results from the lab
  • Incident and overflow reports as PDFs in shared folders
  • Regulator notification dates in someone’s spreadsheet

What that looks like in the demo data:

  • 18 of 43major main breaks have no incident report on file
  • 12overflow reports cover 439 major sewer overflows
  • 12chlorine notices passed their own closure test in April 2026 and were never closed

Synthetic data. The five places are typical of water utilities, not a description of any one organisation.

One desk: see what’s open, read the report, decide what’s next

  • Dashboard

    Open events and missing reports by type, month and region. Every figure covers the period to 12 Nov 2026, the last day of data.

  • Register

    173 major events with the most urgent first, each with the reason it is open. Pick one to act on it.

  • Documents

    Every PDF in the raw volume, read live. New files show up as “not indexed” until someone indexes them.

The data suggests a status. A person decides.

  • Open · suggested

    Dashed means the data proposed it. Example: a storm with 10 of 28 overflow reports on file.

  • Closed

    Filled means a person saved it to Lakebase, with owner, note and regulator-notified date.

  • Every change is kept: who, when, from what, and why. A repeat click can’t duplicate a change.

The report sits beside the record, because every PDF holds a fact no table has

  • A council closed the nearest beach for 48 hours after a storm overflow.
  • A booster chlorinator went in at a reservoir valve, with its dosing log held by the operator.
  • A first call came 34 minutes before dispatch, and the call log doesn’t show it.
  • The PDFs come straight from the volume. Nothing is copied and no permissions are added.

Live demo in five clicks

  1. Dashboard

    161 of 173 events are open, and 135 still need a report or notice. Two chlorine spikes at the start of each summer.

  2. Register → Glenelg catchment storm, 27 Sep 2025

    28 major overflows, 25 of them on pre-1975 sewers with defects. 10 reports on file. Open one and read the beach-closure note.

  3. Port Augusta DMA 3, low chlorine, summer 2025-26

    Its closure test passed on 27 Apr 2026. Click “Accept suggestion: Closed”, and the history records who did it.

  4. Unley DMA 7 leak alert

    Active for 497 days, with night flow 2.2× its peer DMAs. Set an owner, choose “In review” and save.

  5. Documents

    Every PDF in the raw volume. One that lands without an index row is flagged “not indexed”.

How it’s built: Databricks building blocks, one definition per concept

Built in one session by Claude Code as architect, with a data-engineering agent and a gold-layer agent. Lakebase holds every status and its history, written by the app’s own identity. Genie One answers business questions from the same definitions, so its numbers match the app’s.

  • Leak loss is an estimate: inflow × NRW points above peers
